Design Your Creature

If you had a pet that lived inside a computer, what would it need?

What does your creature need to stay healthy?

Think about real animals before you answer. A dog needs food, sleep, and attention. A fish needs water and food. A cat needs food, sleep, and to be left alone at the exact right moments.

Your creature will need things too — and it will be your job to decide what those things are.

Variables: how a program remembers

A computer program can't just "know" that your creature is hungry. Someone has to tell it. And that information has to live somewhere.

Programmers use something called a variable — a container with a name that holds a value. Think of it like a labeled jar.

let food = 80; // 80 out of 100 — not hungry yet
let happy = 70; // 70 out of 100 — pretty happy
let energy = 50; // 50 out of 100 — getting tired

Those three lines are a complete picture of how your creature is doing right now. Every time you feed it, the food number goes up. Every time it runs around, the energy number goes down.

The key idea: a program doesn't feel things — it tracks numbers. Your job as the designer is to decide which numbers matter, and what happens when they get too low.

States: what the numbers mean

A number on its own isn't very interesting. But a number that changes something is.

When food drops below 20, maybe your creature gets droopy eyes. When happy reaches 100, maybe it bounces. When energy hits zero, maybe it falls asleep whether you want it to or not.

These are called states — different conditions your creature can be in, depending on its numbers.

if (food < 20) {
  // creature looks tired and sad
} else if (food > 80) {
  // creature looks full and happy
} else {
  // creature is doing fine
}

The special behavior question

This is the most important question you'll answer today:

What is one thing your creature does that no other creature does?

Not something generic. Not "it jumps." Something that is specific to this creature, with this name.

One kid's creature — a headless rotisserie chicken named Gerald — keeps oracle notes on a napkin. That's not a feature anyone planned. It came from the kid. It made Gerald the most interesting character in the whole project.
